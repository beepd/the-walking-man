About

The Walking Man is a small web page with one idea: a man is walking around the world for peace, and you can watch him do it, live.

What is this?

Open the page and you will see him walking right now, wherever he is on his journey. He leaves one city, walks toward the next, and arrives to a welcome from the locals. Everyone who opens the page at the same moment sees the same thing, so a friend on the other side of the world sees him in the same place you do.

Why peace?

Because the world is mostly made of ordinary people who want the same things: a meal, a safe place to sleep and someone to say hello to. On this walk he eats the local dish in every city, hears the local greeting and moves on to the next. The idea is simple: if you walk far enough and say hello to enough people, the distances between us start to look small.

How does it work?

  • It runs on the clock. His position is worked out from the current time, so there is nothing to refresh and no one controlling him.
  • He keeps a daily routine. He walks from 6 am, has breakfast, lunch, a snack and dinner, walks until 10 pm and then sleeps in a tent by a campfire. Times follow the local sun wherever he is.
  • He walks at 5 km/h and rows a boat at 3.5 km/h across the sea. At that pace, one trip around all 36 cities takes about 1,000 days.
  • The walk began on Sunday 18 October 2026 from Mumbai, and it keeps going round and round.

You can see every stop, with its date, local dish and greeting, on the route page.

Who made it?

One person, as a side project, for the fun of it. The artwork, sounds and animation are all made in code, with no stock images and no video.

Tips

  • Turn on sound with the speaker button at the top right: footsteps, waves at sea, crickets and a crackling fire at night.
  • It looks best on a phone held sideways.
  • Come back at different times of day. He might be eating lunch in Tokyo or sleeping by a fire in Lima.
